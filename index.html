Every read here assumes one pace A firm, holing-out stroke — the ball finishing about a foot (30 cm) past on a mid-length putt. That pace is baked into the divisor. Own it and the numbers are right; vary it and they drift.
Dying it in at the lipadd a third
A foot past — the defaultas shown
Ramming it, two feet pasttake off a fifth

Speed sets the read, not the other way round. A dying putt spends its last metre almost stationary, so gravity gets far longer to work — nearly double the break of a firm one. Two players with different pace need different aim points, and both are right.

  1. Circle the hole at four or five paces, always facing the hole.
  2. At 3 and 9 the ground runs across you — one foot is clearly lower. Maximum discomfort.
  3. Keep going until your feet feel level. That happens twice, and both times you are on the fall line.
  4. Tell the two apart with your shins: leaning back means you are below the hole at 6. Leaning forward means above it at 12.
  5. That line through 12 and 6 is the straight putt. A ball anywhere on it has no break.

The badge always stands upright so you can read it; the bar through it is your stance — it swings round as you circle, and the two white dots are your feet.

Why it works: your feet only feel the slope running across them. Facing straight up or straight down the fall line puts all of it front-to-back, where your feet cannot sense it — but your shins can.
